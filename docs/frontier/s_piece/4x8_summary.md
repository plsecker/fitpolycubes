# S-Pentacube 4×8×z Macro Analysis — Summary

## Executive summary

The 4×8×z S-pentacube analysis has produced a strong structural result rather than merely a larger collection of individual tilings.

The Macro/state-graph analysis found a recurrent strongly connected component (SCC) of 478 states containing two fundamental recurrent cycle structures of lengths **20** and **130**. All 16,419 analysed cycles in that SCC have lengths in the semigroup generated by 20 and 130, and all are divisible by 10. The two primitive cycle structures are the 20-cycle and the 130-cycle; the observed 40, 60, 140, 150 and 160 cycles are composite combinations of these.

The 20-cycle gives a very fast generative construction for arbitrarily long multiples of 20. The 130-cycle is the genuinely new recurrent structure and changes the eventual arithmetic from an apparent period of 20 to a period of 10, since

\[
\gcd(20,130)=10.
\]

This yields an **unconditional infinite family**: every 4×8×z box with \(z\ge120\) and \(z\equiv0\pmod{10}\) is tileable. Explicit constructions have also been validated for z = 140.

The main remaining caveat is the necessity direction. The analysed 478-state SCC is self-contained (all outgoing transitions from SCC states remain within the SCC, and state 0 lies in it), but the larger 30M-state Macro closure from which the SCC was obtained was capped. Therefore the evidence strongly supports the global classification, but the statement that *no additional tileable residue class can exist outside the explored closure* is not yet an independent global proof.

---

## 1. Original objective

The initial target was to complete the analysis of the S-pentacube for boxes of the form

\[
4\times8\times z,
\]

first for \(z<130\), and then to push beyond 130.

The work initially focused on whether Shirakawa's published existence data could be extended or made exhaustive. The Macro method turned out to be more useful as a structural state-graph method than as a one-off enumerator.

---

## 2. Complete 4×8 existence classification below 130

Using the existing 15M/30M Macro and SCC data, all relevant 4×8×z cases below 130 were classified for existence.

### Tileable values

\[
\boxed{z\in\{20,40,60,80,100,120\}}
\]

### Impossible values

All other admissible z values below 130 were ruled out in the analysed Macro/SCC structure. In particular:

- 5, 10, 15 — impossible
- 25, 30, 35 — impossible
- 45, 50, 55 — impossible
- 65, 70, 75 — impossible
- 85, 90 — impossible
- 95 — impossible
- 105, 110, 115 — impossible
- 125 — impossible

The 20 case was independently exhaustively verified as unique.

The important point is that the cases 40–120 do **not** represent fundamentally new tiling structures: they can be generated by repeating the verified 20-cycle.

---

## 3. The Macro/SCC structure

The key state-graph result came from the existing 4×8 Macro closure and SCC analysis.

### SCC

The recurrent SCC contains **478 states**.

The analysis found:

- 16,419 directed cycles
- cycle lengths observed: 20, 40, 60, 130, 140, 150, 160
- gcd of the cycle lengths: 10
- exactly two primitive cycle structures:
  - 20-cycle
  - 130-cycle

All other observed cycles are composite combinations of these primitive cycles.

The SCC is also a **trap**:

- state 0 lies in the SCC;
- every transition out of an SCC state remains inside the SCC.

Therefore any Macro path from state 0 back to state 0 that enters this SCC cannot leave it.

This makes the SCC structure especially important for understanding long-run/recurrent tilings.

---

## 4. The 20-cycle

The 20-cycle is the fundamental recurring construction already visible in the small solutions.

It gives a direct generative mechanism:

\[
20,40,60,80,100,120,140,160,\ldots
\]

by repeating the cycle.

This is why the known 40–120 cases do not by themselves constitute new structural discoveries: they are straightforward concatenations of the 20-thick construction.

A concrete 4×8×140 tiling was generated by repeating the 20-cycle seven times and independently validated:

- 140 Macro edges;
- 896 S-pentacube placements;
- 4,480 cells covered;
- no overlap or gaps;
- bounds exactly 4×8×140.

This demonstrates that the rapid generative construction continues beyond 130.

---

## 5. The 130-cycle

The 130-cycle is the genuinely new recurrent structure.

Shirakawa already lists 4×8×130 as a tileable case, so **130 is not itself a new box size discovery**. Its significance is structural: it introduces a cycle length not generated by the 20-cycle alone.

Since

\[
130 = 6\times20 + 10,
\]

we obtain

\[
\gcd(20,130)=10.
\]

This changes the long-run arithmetic from an apparent period 20 to an eventual period 10.

The 130-cycle was explicitly extracted and verified in the SCC analysis. The 4×8×130 computation also produced 2,048 tilings within the 30M closure, followed by symmetry reduction of those solutions using the validated 8-element box symmetry group.

The 2,048 count is exact **for the analysed 30M closure**, but not yet a global proof that no additional 4×8×130 tilings exist outside that closure.

---

## 6. Infinite-family theorem: unconditional direction

The combination of the 20-cycle and 130-cycle gives an explicit constructive infinite family.

Because

\[
\gcd(20,130)=10,
\]

and the Frobenius-number argument gives all sufficiently large multiples of 10, every multiple of 10 at or above 120 can be written as

\[
20a+130b,
\qquad a,b\ge0.
\]

Therefore the existing verified cycles give explicit tiling constructions for

\[
\boxed{z\ge120,\quad z\equiv0\pmod{10}}.
\]

This is **unconditional sufficiency**: it relies only on explicit verified cycles and cycle insertion, not on assuming that the explored closure contains every possible state.

Examples include:

\[
120,130,140,150,160,170,\ldots
\]

all as members of the constructive family.

---

## 7. Necessity and the remaining caveat

The stronger statement would be:

> 4×8×z is tileable iff z is one of the explicitly allowed small cases or, beyond the threshold, z is a multiple of 10.

The current SCC analysis strongly supports this, but the necessity direction has one technical gap.

### What is established

Within the analysed 478-state SCC:

- all relevant return-path lengths have the required mod-10 structure;
- the SCC is self-contained;
- state 0 is inside the SCC;
- all analysed cycle lengths are divisible by 10.

The analysed reachable distances satisfy the corresponding residue restriction.

### What is not independently established

The 30M-state Macro closure used to obtain the SCC was capped. Therefore we have not independently proved that **no other state or recurrent component exists outside the explored closure** that could generate a different return-length residue.

A universal L1-even invariant was found and proved to be structural, but it was not sufficient on its own to derive the missing factor of 2 in the path-length condition.

Thus the safest current statement is:

> The infinite family for z≥120, z≡0 (mod 10) is proved unconditionally. The corresponding “only if” statement is extremely strongly supported by the analysed SCC, but its global necessity proof still depends on completeness of the relevant state space (or on finding an independent universal invariant).

---

## 8. What was learned from the generalized Macro work

The Macro method is not inherently a 4×8-only trick. A generalized finite-cross-section prototype was validated on other cross-sections, including:

- 4×5: 1,538 states
- 5×6: about 7.9M states

However, the 4×9 experiment demonstrated that scaling can become very expensive.

A 4×9 run reached:

- 10M first-generation states;
- 10M Macro states;
- 10,010,721 Macro edges;
- 280,843,521 intermediate states;
- about 43.8 minutes;

before hitting the artificial cap.

This established that generalized Macro is powerful but that the state-space size is highly geometry-dependent. That work is now parked; the priority is the 4×8 structural result.

---

## 9. Checkpoint/resume infrastructure

The generalized Macro implementation now supports portable checkpoint/resume, including:

- arbitrary-precision state serialization;
- restoration of discovered states and work queues;
- preservation of run statistics;
- atomic checkpoint writes;
- SIGINT/SIGTERM checkpoint handling;
- restart without recomputation.

The mechanism was successfully tested across machines using a compressed checkpoint transferred through the VirtualBox shared folder.

This infrastructure is useful for future large Macro searches even though it is not needed for the current 4×8 infinite-family construction.

---

## 10. Relationship to Shirakawa

The work does **not** currently amount to discovering a new previously unknown 4×8×z box size in the Shirakawa catalogue.

In particular:

- 20 was already known;
- 130 was already known;
- 40–120 are generated directly by repeating the 20-cycle;
- 140 and later multiples of 10 can be generated from the established cycle structure.

The novelty is instead structural and computational:

1. a complete small-range existence classification for 4×8×z below 130;
2. a fast state-graph/generative mechanism for producing arbitrarily long tilings;
3. identification of the 20- and 130-cycle structures as the two primitive recurrent structures found in the analysed SCC;
4. the resulting unconditional infinite family of 4×8×z tilings for all z≥120 divisible by 10.

The next genuinely new discovery would therefore likely require a **new irreducible/prime structure**, rather than another concatenation of the existing 20/130 machinery.

---

## 11. Current status

### Established

- Complete 4×8 existence classification for z<130 in the analysed Macro framework.
- Explicit 20-cycle.
- Explicit 130-cycle.
- 20- and 130-cycle gcd = 10.
- Two primitive recurrent cycle structures in the analysed 478-state SCC.
- Unconditional infinite family for z≥120, z≡0 (mod 10).
- Explicit validated 4×8×140 tiling.
- Fast generative construction for arbitrary members of the known family.
- 4×8×130: 2,048 tilings found within the 30M closure and symmetry-reduced.

### Open / qualified

- Global proof that the analysed 478-state SCC is the complete relevant state space.
- Therefore, the universal “only multiples of 10” necessity statement remains conditional.
- The global exact count of all 4×8×130 tilings remains unproved beyond the 30M closure.
- Exact counts for the concatenated 40–120 cases are secondary and remain unfinished.

### Strategic direction

Do not spend major compute enumerating the already-generated 40–120 or trivial 140/150/160 family members.

The interesting next research targets are:

- proving the necessity side globally without a huge closure expansion, or
- finding genuinely new irreducible S-pentacube structures beyond the existing 20/130 recurrent semigroup, potentially in a different cross-section or through a new prime box.

## Bottom line

The main result is no longer “we found another tiling.”

The Macro analysis has exposed a compact recurrent structure for the 4×8 S-pentacube:

\[
\boxed{20\text{-cycle} + 130\text{-cycle} \Rightarrow \gcd=10}
\]

and hence a fast, explicit, indefinitely extendable family of tilings for

\[
\boxed{4\times8\times z,\quad z\ge120,\ z\equiv0\pmod{10}}.
\]

The remaining question is whether the same structure can be promoted from a highly supported computational classification to a fully global theorem, or whether the next real breakthrough will come from finding a genuinely new irreducible structure outside the current 4×8 recurrent semigroup.
