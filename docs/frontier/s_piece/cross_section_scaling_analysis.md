# Cross-Section Scaling Analysis: 4×9 ↔ 5×9 and the Fivefold Hypothesis

**Date**: 2026-08-25  
**Status**: COMPLETE — The fivefold scaling is a numerical coincidence, not a structural transformation.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The observation that 4×9 generators {60,75,90,105} are exactly 5× the 5×9 generators {12,15,18,21} raised the question of whether the two Macro graphs are structurally related. This investigation finds:

**The fivefold scaling is a numerical coincidence, not a structural transformation.** Only the cycle length itself scales by 5. Every other quantity (edge pieces, frontier weights, z-spans, orientation counts) scales differently or not at all. No state/transition correspondence exists between the two Macro graphs.

### 2.3 Edge-level comparison

When 4×9×60 edges are grouped in blocks of 5 (to match one 5×9×12 edge), the summed piece counts do NOT match:

```
4×9×60 edges grouped by 5: [44, 33, 37, 35, 37, 35, 36, 36, 46, 32, 36]
5×9×12 edges:               [17,  6, 10,  9, 11,  5, 12,  7, 10,  9, 12]
```

**Zero exact matches** in any of the 11 edge groups. The grouped sums are 2-3× larger than the corresponding 5×9 edges, not equal.

### 2.4 Frontier state comparison

The 4×9×60 and 5×9×12 cycles share **6 common frontier weights** out of a possible 40 and 11 unique weights respectively:

Common: (0,0,0), (17,8,0), (23,2,0), (24,6,0), (25,10,0), (32,8,0)

These are common frontier motifs that appear across **many** cross-sections, not specific to 4×9 or 5×9. After scaling L0 by the area ratio (45/36 = 1.25), only 4 weights match, including the trivial (0,0,0) and (45,0,0) = GATE.

### 2.5 Orientation distribution

The orientation cosine similarity between 4×9 and 5×9 cycles is 0.96-0.98, but this is **comparable to within-cross-section similarity**:

| Comparison | Similarity range |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Within 4×9 (60 vs 75 vs 90 vs 105) | 0.979-0.997 |
| Within 5×9 (12 vs 15 vs 18 vs 21) | 0.926-0.979 |
| **Between 4×9 and 5×9** | **0.956-0.984** |

The between-group similarity is not higher than the within-group similarity. The orientation distributions are broadly similar across **all** cyclic cross-sections, suggesting a general "S pentacube natural orientation palette" rather than a specific 4×9↔5×9 relationship.

## 3. Why the Same Scaled Semigroup?

Both cross-sections have the scaled semigroup ⟨4,5,6,7⟩:

- 4×9: 60 = 4×15, 75 = 5×15, 90 = 6×15, 105 = 7×15
- 5×9: 12 = 4×3, 15 = 5×3, 18 = 6×3, 21 = 7×3

The scaled semigroup ⟨4,5,6,7⟩ is the semigroup generated by **4 consecutive integers starting at 4**. This is a simple numerical coincidence: both cross-sections happen to have exactly 4 catalogue primes that are equally spaced by their GCD.

The spacing equals the GCD:
- 4×9 GCD = 15, spacing = 15
- 5×9 GCD = 3, spacing = 3

The fact that both have exactly 4 primes is a property of the catalogue, not of the Macro graph structure.

## 6. Conclusion Classification

### VERIFIED FACT
1. **Only cycle length scales by 5** between 4×9 and 5×9. No other quantity scales by 5.
2. **No state/transition correspondence exists** between 4×9 and 5×9 cycles. Grouped edge sums do not match, frontier weights do not correspond, and no mapping can be constructed.
3. **The scaled semigroup ⟨4,5,6,7⟩ is identical** for both 4×9 and 5×9, but this is a consequence of both having 4 equally-spaced catalogue primes.
4. **Area-40 cycles share z-span and layer occupancy** — a geometric consequence of same area.

### STRONG OBSERVATION
1. **Orientation distributions are broadly similar across all cyclic cross-sections** (cosine sim 0.83-0.99). This suggests a general "S pentacube natural orientation palette" rather than any specific cross-section relationship.
2. **The 4×9 and 5×9 orientation similarity (0.96-0.98) is comparable to within-cross-section similarity** — it does not indicate a specific structural relationship.

### HYPOTHESIS (REFUTED)
1. **The 4×9 Macro graph is a "stretched" version of the 5×9 Macro graph.** REFUTED: No edge-level or state-level correspondence exists.
2. **The scaling factor equals the area ratio.** REFUTED: The scaling factor is 5, not 1.25 (the area ratio).
3. **There is a general principle relating Macro graphs of different cross-sections.** REFUTED: No consistent pattern across neighbouring cross-sections.

## 10. Best Next Mathematical Question

**Why do different cross-sections have such similar orientation distributions?** 

The orientation cosine similarity between any pair of cyclic cross-sections ranges from 0.83 to 0.99, despite vastly different cycle lengths and frontier structures. This suggests the S pentacube has a "natural" orientation palette that is largely independent of the box shape. Understanding this phenomenon could reveal deeper structure in the Macro model.

Specific questions:
1. Is the orientation distribution determined entirely by the template set?
2. Does every cycle in a given cross-section have the same orientation distribution?
3. Can we predict the orientation distribution from the cross-section dimensions alone?
