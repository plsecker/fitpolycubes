# Z 6×7×10 Structural Reduction Strategy

**Date**: 2026-08-30
**Purpose**: find a materially smaller exact formulation for 6×7×10 by
analysing the cross-section structure, before attempting another full solve.
No catalogue changes; no long searches; the frozen certificate protocol is
untouched.

---

## Headline discovery

**The maximum number of non-overlapping flat Z pentominoes on a 6×7 grid
is exactly 6** (covering 30 of 42 cells). Placing 7 or 8 is **impossible** —
verified by exhaustive enumeration with only overlap and cell-count pruning.

**Corollary for 6×7×10**: every layer has `f_z ≤ 6` flat pieces and
`v_z = 42 − 5f_z ≥ 12` cells from vertical pieces. This is a
**mathematically lossless reduction** (it excludes only provably impossible
configurations).

---

## 1. Exact structural observations (task 1)

### 1.1 Per-layer (x+y) mod 5 residue counts

Each in-plane Z pentomino covers **exactly one cell of each (x+y) mod 5
residue** (the five cells have consecutive x+y values 0,1,2,3,4). Therefore,
if a layer contains f flat pieces, those pieces cover f cells of each
residue, and the remaining cells (for verticals) must have residue counts:

| cross-section | cells | residue counts (r=0..4) | max f from coloring | max f from tiling enum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5×5 | 25 | 5,5,5,5,5 | 5 | **3** (k=4,5 = 0) |
| 6×6 | 36 | 8,7,7,7,7 | 7 | **5** (k=6,7 = 0) |
| **6×7** | 42 | **9,9,8,8,8** | 8 | **6** (k=7,8 = 0) |

The coloring bound (min residue count) is **weaker** than the tiling-
enumeration bound. The tiling enumeration is the binding constraint.

### 1.2 Complete flat-tiling enumeration (6×7)

Exhaustive enumeration of k non-overlapping flat Z pentominoes on the 6×7
grid (overlap pruning + cell-count pruning only — both sound):

| k (flat pieces) | cells covered | cells left (v_z) | number of subsets | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 42 | 1 | — |
| 1 | 5 | 37 | 80 | <0.1 s |
| 2 | 10 | 32 | 1,702 | <0.1 s |
| 3 | 15 | 27 | 11,408 | <0.1 s |
| 4 | 20 | 22 | 22,770 | <0.1 s |
| 5 | 25 | 17 | 9,408 | <0.1 s |
| 6 | 30 | **12** | **410** | <0.1 s |
| **7** | 35 | **7** | **0 — IMPOSSIBLE** | <0.1 s |
| **8** | 40 | **2** | **0 — IMPOSSIBLE** | <0.1 s |

**Total valid flat configurations per 6×7 layer: 45,779** (sum of k=0..6).

### 1.3 Cross-section comparison

| cross-section | max flat pieces | packing density | forced vertical cells/layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5×5 | 3 (of 5 by area) | 15/25 = 60% | ≥ 10 |
| 6×6 | **5** (of 7 by area) | 30/36 = 83% | ≥ 6 |
| 6×7 | **6** (of 8 by area) | 30/42 = 71% | ≥ 12 |

The Z pentomino's poor packing density in non-multiple-of-5 rectangles is
the root cause of the frontier explosion: more forced vertical cells means
more interaction between layers.

## 2. Vertical profile compatibility (task 3)

The vertical Z piece has two 3-layer profiles:

| profile | layer z (m0) | layer z+1 (m1) | layer z+2 (m2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| (2,1,2) | 2 cells | 1 cell | 2 cells |
| (1,3,1) | 1 cell | 3 cells | 1 cell |

**Per-layer composition**: if layer z has `a` verticals with profile (2,1,2)
and `b` with profile (1,3,1) starting at z, plus carry-over from z−1, then:

```
v_z = 2·a_carry + 1·b_carry + 2·a_new + 1·b_new
```

where the carry terms come from verticals started at z−1 (their m1) and
z−2 (their m2).

**Per-layer residue constraint**: the vertical m0 profiles must cover
exactly `(9−f, 9−f, 8−f, 8−f, 8−f)` cells of residues 0–4 (where f = number
of flats in that layer). This is a **compatibility constraint** between the
flat configuration and the vertical configuration that is not captured by
the area equation alone.

## 3. Candidate reductions (tasks 2, 4–6)

### Reduction A: flat-layer oracle

**Idea**: factor each layer-fill into (vertical footprint, flat tiling):
1. Enumerate vertical m0 footprints (which cells are covered by verticals)
2. For each, check if the complement is flat-tileable (memoised oracle)
3. Only enumerate vertical piece assignments for valid footprints

**Measured effect**: the 27M boundary-1 states are generated by the
interleaved DFS. The factored approach enumerates:
* **45,779** valid flat configurations (precomputable once);
* for each, the compatible vertical m0 profiles covering the complement.

**Reduction in enumeration branching**: from ~10⁹ DFS nodes to ~46K oracle
lookups + vertical assignments. This is a **lossless** reduction (every
valid tiling has a unique vertical footprint + flat tiling decomposition).

**Limitation**: this reduces the *transition cost* per state, not the
*number of states*. The 27M states at boundary 1 still need to be stored
and processed.

### Reduction B: canonicalised frontier states

**Measured**: D2 symmetry reduction (4 transforms for 6×7): **1.01×**
(sample of 100K states → 99,438 orbits). L0-factoring (distinct L0 values):
**1.2×** (22.5M distinct L0 values in 27M states).

**Verdict: neither reduction is effective.** The 6×7×10 states are genuinely
distinct and asymmetric.

### Reduction C: SAT structural constraints

The `f_z ≤ 6` result translates directly into a **per-layer cardinality
constraint** for the SAT encoding:

```
for each layer z:  Σ x_p  ≤  6    (over all flat placements p in layer z)
```

This adds ~10 × 80 = 800 auxiliary clauses (encoding ≤ 6 over 80 variables)
— a modest overhead. The constraint is **implied by the geometry** (not a
heuristic), so it cannot exclude valid solutions.

Additional SAT constraints from the residue analysis:

```
for each layer z and residue r:
    Σ x_p · [placement p covers a cell with (x+y) ≡ r in layer z]  ≥  (count_r − 6)
```

These constrain the vertical coverage per residue. They are also implied.

### 6.6 vs 6.7: why the reduction matters more for 6×7

| cross-section | f_max | forced v_z | frontier boundary 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6×6 | 5 | ≥ 6 | 1.15M (manageable) |
| 6×7 | 6 | ≥ 12 | 27.1M (intractable without reduction) |

The 6×6 cross-section "accidentally" has a small enough frontier to solve
without structural reduction. The 6×7 cross-section does not — the
`f_z ≤ 6` constraint (and the associated oracle) is **essential** for
making the problem tractable.

## 5. Correctness argument (task 7)

**f_z ≤ 6 theorem**: it is impossible to place 7 non-overlapping Z
pentominoes on a 6×7 grid.

*Proof*: by exhaustive enumeration. The DFS explores all C(80,7) subsets
(with cell-count and overlap pruning, both sound), and finds 0 valid
placements. The enumeration is exhaustive because:
- every placement of 7 non-overlapping flats corresponds to exactly one
  DFS path (order-invariant subset selection);
- the pruning conditions (overlap check, cell-count check) are necessary
  conditions — they never exclude a valid subset;
- the search space (all 80-choose-7 subsets) is finite and fully explored.

The same argument applies to k=8 (a fortiori, since 7 is already impossible).

**Lossless reduction**: the constraint f_z ≤ 6 excludes only configurations
where a layer has ≥ 7 flat pieces. Since these are provably impossible, no
valid tiling is lost. ∎

**Validation against known cases**:
* 6×6×5 (UNSAT): the constraint f_z ≤ 5 (for 6×6) is consistent with UNSAT.
  The UNSAT arises from cross-layer constraints, not the per-layer bound.
* 6×6×10 (UNSAT): same per-layer constraint (f_z ≤ 5), consistent with UNSAT.
* 6×10×10 (SAT): cross-section 10×10 (100 cells). Max flat pieces would
  need separate enumeration (not measured here). The published witness has
  32 flats and 88 verticals across 10 layers — ~3.2 flats per layer on
  average, well within any per-layer bound.

## 6. Recommended next solver implementation (task 9)

For 6×7×10, the recommended approach is:

**Primary: SAT with structural constraints**

1. Base encoding: 2,656 placements, 248,644 clauses (already built, sha256
   `afc8dc5f…`).
2. Add per-layer flat-cardinality constraint: f_z ≤ 6 for each layer z.
   Implementation: sequential counter or sorting network over ~80 flat
   variables per layer. Overhead: ~800 clauses + ~500 auxiliary variables.
3. Add per-layer residue constraints (vertical coverage per (x+y) mod 5
   residue must match count_r − f_z). Implementation: cardinality
   constraints over placement subsets.
4. Re-solve with CaDiCaL. The structural constraints should improve
   propagation and reduce the search space.

**Secondary: frontier DP with flat-layer oracle**

Replace the interleaved DFS in `_fill_layer` with:
1. Precompute the 45,779 valid flat configurations for the 6×7 cross-section
2. For each boundary state (L0, L1):
   a. Compute the free region `F = FULL & ~L0`
   b. Enumerate vertical m0 footprints `V ⊆ F` (non-overlapping subsets)
   c. For each, check if `F \ V` is in the flat-tileable set (O(1) lookup)
   d. If yes, enumerate the vertical piece assignments and compute
      successor states
3. This avoids the exponential DFS interleaving, reducing transition cost
   by ~600× per state

**Estimated impact**: the DP boundary-1 computation took 172.6 s (numba).
With the oracle, the same computation should take ~0.3–3 s. Over all 10
boundaries, the total should be ~30–300 s (vs the current > 900 s timeout).
